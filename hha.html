Home Health Aide (HHA) Training Program

Learn to care for people safely and with dignity in their own homes. Our 3-week program in Claymont, Delaware, prepares you to work for Delaware licensed home health agencies.

What you will learn

  • Personal care and activities of daily living
  • Homemaker services and medication reminders
  • Nutrition and hydration
  • Infection control
  • Observing, reporting and documenting patient status
  • Keeping a clean, safe and healthy home
  • Communication skills
  • Taking temperature, pulse and respiration
  • Safe transfers, walking assistance and positioning
  • Range of motion and body mechanics
  • Caring for older adults and people with disabilities
  • Patient rights, confidentiality, and abuse and neglect prevention
  • Dementia care
  • Recognizing and responding to emergencies

The curriculum meets Delaware home health aide training requirements (16 Del. Admin. Code 3350 and 3351) and federal standards (42 CFR 484.80). Textbook: Providing Home Care, 7th edition (Hartman Publishing).

How the 3 weeks work

  1. Days 1 to 3

    Classroom

    18 hours of instruction before any hands-on practice.

  2. Days 4 to 10

    Classroom and skills lab

    42 hours practicing on real equipment: hospital bed, mechanical lift, wheelchair, walker, vital-signs tools and more.

  3. Days 11 to 13

    Supervised practical training

    16 hours performing home health aide tasks under the direct supervision of a nurse, ending with your competency evaluation.

Two make-up days are built into every cohort, so a snow day never costs you your certificate.

Five requirements to enroll

  • At least 18 years old
  • High school diploma, GED or equivalent
  • Valid government-issued photo ID
  • Criminal background check through DELJIS
  • Drug screening

A criminal history does not automatically disqualify you. Results are reviewed case by case, in line with state rules and the hiring standards of Delaware home health agencies.

What you get

  • A Certificate of Completion showing you finished a home health aide training program of at least 75 hours, as Delaware requires
  • CPR training, your textbook, uniform and supplies, all included in the program cost
  • Resume help, interview practice and job placement assistance

Good to know: Delaware does not issue a state certification or registry listing for home health aides. Your certificate shows employers you completed the required training; each agency then gives its own skills check and orientation before you start.
